Knowing when not to trust yourself

Retrieval-based knowledge editors route each query by thresholding a verifier's confidence. That breaks when the verifier is confidently wrong — and no recalibration of that confidence can detect it. Measuring whether confidence is discriminative, rather than how high it is, fixes the failure and transfers to a dataset the model never saw.

0.956 AUROC on a held-out dataset never seen in training
+15.9 points where fixed gating fails (p=0.0019)
0 decisions changed where fixed gating already works

The failure, and why thresholds can't fix it

A router scores the top retrieved candidate with a cross-encoder and thresholds it: decline below one cut, recite the stored fact above another, otherwise reason over the evidence. On MQuAKE-CF this gating is net-negative in every input mode — worse than simply disabling the gates:

Two natural fixes fail, and their failure is the diagnosis:

  • Recalibrating the threshold moves the false-fire rate 18.33% → 18.22%. The score distributions for correct and incorrect retrievals genuinely overlap.
  • Raising the threshold 0.85 → 0.97 changes nothing — byte-identical decisions. Confidence on the wrong retrievals is already above 0.97.

The router is asking the wrong question. It asks “how confident is the verifier?” when it needs “is this confidence discriminative?” A verifier scoring 0.99 on the top card and 0.98 on four unrelated ones isn't confident — it's saturated, and its pick is close to arbitrary.

Method

Predict P(top-1 retrieval is correct) from the shape of the top-k retrieval and verification result rather than its top-1 magnitude. Fifteen features; the load-bearing ones are second-order: margin to the best competitor, how many candidates clear the threshold, softmax entropy over the neighbourhood, distinct subjects among the top-k.

The constraint that makes this a method and not a per-dataset switch: no feature is dataset identity, and no dataset-level statistic is available at inference. The head is trained on CounterFact + WikiUpdate with MQuAKE-CF held out entirely, so it must generalise to a regime it has never seen.

Linear (logistic regression), chosen over higher capacity deliberately: small training set, the claim rests on out-of-distribution transfer, and the coefficients are inspectable so the mechanism is checkable rather than asserted. A gradient-boosted model on the same features scores slightly better out of distribution (0.967 vs 0.956) — reported because it doesn't flatter the choice.

Results

One head, one threshold, no per-dataset tuning. Paired tests throughout: exact McNemar on discordant pairs, paired bootstrap, Wilson intervals, Holm–Bonferroni across cells.

What the head does behaviourally

  • Bypass fires at 27.0% on MQuAKE-CF extracted and 0.0% on CounterFact structured — same head, same threshold.
  • On both structured cells it produces zero discordant pairs with fixed gating: identical decisions on every query, not merely matched accuracy.
  • Accuracy is unchanged for every threshold from 0.3 to 0.8 while the bypass rate moves — not a knife-edge result.
  • The +15.87 gain is identical at 1.5B and 7B. Router inputs are generator-independent, so the mechanism is architectural.

A refinement we proposed and refuted Negative result

WikiUpdate's unreliable cases score lower than MQuAKE-CF's (0.62–0.66 vs 0.75–0.76), suggesting the magnitude of predicted unreliability could separate “misleading, decline” from “imperfect, reason over it.” We added a second lower threshold and tested it.

It failed on the cell it was designed to fix (43.13% → 41.25%) and was catastrophic on MQuAKE-CF (80.95% → 58.73%), below even the original gating. The hypothesis reasoned from means while ignoring distributions.

The failure is more useful than success would have been: it exposes a target mismatch. The head predicts P(retrieval correct), but the router needs P(this action yields a correct answer) — and those come apart. A wrong retrieval can still be answered correctly from parametric knowledge; a right one can be misread. Tellingly, the old verifier-threshold REJECT set is a worse predictor of retrieval correctness yet a better-chosen action set on WikiUpdate.

Next step, now properly motivated: train on outcome labels — for each query and each action, whether it produced a correct answer — and select the argmax. Decision-theoretic rather than retrieval-quality-based; subsumes the current policy as a special case.

Limitations

  • MQuAKE-CF slices are n=63; the structured-mode result cannot reach significance.
  • One retrieval stack (MiniLM + one cross-encoder); sensitivity to that choice is untested.
  • Transfer demonstrated on one held-out dataset, not many.
  • Cost: k cross-encoder calls per query instead of 1 (k=5).
  • Not a weight-editing replacement. On clean structured single-fact edits, weight editors remain competitive; the claim here is about robustness across input conditions and retrieval regimes.
